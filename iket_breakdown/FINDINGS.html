Kernel-only conclusions

  • The kernel is at dg parity ≤128 tok/rank (0.97–1.04×) and wins outright ≥512 (0.94× → 0.64× at 8k). The only kernel-level slowdown is a hump peaked exactly at 256 tok/rank (+21%) — not a trend.
  • IKR ruled out.job 2359949 MEGA_IKR=1 is worse at every point in the window (+12–16%); its cross-rank REDG atomic-adds cost more than the staged combine (epi_fc2 71.0 → 113.5 µs at 256).
  • ~40% of the hump is a knob-table gap. Online autotune recovers 16.4 µs at 256; the winning change in the 256-max-token bucket is token_back_mode: epi_warps → standalone_warps (combine push moved to a dedicated warp group), tiler unchanged.
  • Smoking gun: the combine store is anomalous only in the 256 bucket. fc2_store_combine work (epilogue_refactor.py:2349) = 69 µs at 256 but 29 µs at 384 — non-monotonic in tokens ⇒ a configuration artifact of the epi-warps token-back at that pool geometry, not a per-token cost.
  • Fixed floor (absolute latency at tiny tokens, parity-neutral): tail NVLink quiesce ~23–26 µs (Tail.NvlinkDrain token_comm.py:1865 + Tail.NvlinkPublish :1931), dispatch count-exchange ~14 µs (Dispatch_Barrier :1613), fc1 weight-streaming floor ~60–70 µs (mma_fc1 kernel_fc12.py:2324) that both backends pay.
  • Exonerated: sched_publish (~55%) and epi_fc1_wait (~45%) are pacing symptoms of the fc1 weight stream; Sched_PreInit_Wait ≈ 0.

3 · Where kernel time goes (rank 0, phase timing)

The mega kernel is one fused launch that does the whole MoE layer: tokens are exchanged between GPUs, run through both expert GEMMs, and combined back — with no separate kernels in between. Inside each CTA, 12 warps are split into five specialized roles that form a pipeline:

rolewarpsjob
dispatchw8–11pull this rank's share of tokens from all peers over NVLink into the local pool
schedw7turn arrived tokens into work tiles and hand them to the consumers
TMA A/Bw5–6stream expert weights (A) and token activations (B) into shared memory
MMAw4run the fc1 and fc2 tensor-core GEMMs
epiloguew0–3SwiGLU + re-quantize after fc1; after fc2, store results back to the token's home rank (the combine)

How to read the bars: each bar is the time one role spent in one phase, measured on that role's own clock (a %clock64 range around the same code the IKET markers wrap). Because all roles run concurrently, the bars overlap in time and do not sum to the kernel total — a big blue bar means that role was doing real work; a big orange bar means it sat waiting (idle, spinning on a counter, or at a barrier). Waits are mirrors: when the epilogue's epi_fc1_wait is large it's because mma_fc1 upstream is the pacer, and when the scheduler's sched_publish is large it's because the consumers downstream aren't draining tiles. So the question to ask per chart is: which blue bar is the pacer that all the orange bars are waiting on?

4 · Deferred: e2e / barrier-cold behavior (not kernel)

  • e2e (barrier-cold full FI forward) runs 1.19–1.36× at 8–256 tok/rank, crossing below 1.0 between 512 and 1024 — a constant ~40–60 µs/launch cost dg does not pay (dg e2e−kernel ≈ 8–25 µs; cutedsl ≈ 48–87 µs, roughly constant through 8k).
  • Mechanism proof (job 2359944, e2e mode + phase timing): at 8 tok/rank Dispatch_Barrier inflates 14 µs → 164 µs (75% of kernel) on rank 0 — per-rank host launch jitter is absorbed at the first cross-rank sync point.
