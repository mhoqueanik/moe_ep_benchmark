Why nvfp4_cutedsl loses to deep_gemm_mega at low tokens/rank

IKET-marker phase breakdown via the clock64 fallback · SLURM jobs 2359277 + 2359364 · 1×8 B200, EP8, 256 experts top-8, hidden 7168, inter 2048, cutlass-dsl 4.5.2 · flashinfer branch iket_analysis

Exact causes

  • Kernel-tail NVLink quiesce — ~23–26 µs of fixed cost on every launch.primary
    Markers Tail.NvlinkDrain (~15–18 µs, src/src/token_comm.py:1865) and Tail.NvlinkPublish (~8 µs, token_comm.py:1931): a 3-barrier sense-reversing NVLink sequence + counter resets ends every launch. 15% of the entire kernel at 8 tok/rank.
  • Dispatch count-exchange barrier — ~14 µs of fixed cost.primary
    Marker Dispatch_Barrier (token_comm.py:1613): all-rank expert-count exchange before any token moves.
  • Cold-launch arrival skew multiplies both — the ~35–50 µs e2e-vs-kernel delta.primary
    Back-to-back (thunk) launches: cutedsl = dg. Barrier-cold launches: cutedsl pays +48 µs over its own kernel time, dg only +8–10 µs — a kernel with 4 cross-rank sync points inherits the slowest rank's launch delay at each one. Host-side FI forward prep is what staggers arrival.
  • fc2 combine peer-STG — linear per-token cost, ~20% of kernel at scale.secondary
    Marker fc2_store_combine (epilogue_refactor.py:2349), wait split via fc2_epi_wait: work grows 29 µs @128 → 72 µs @256 → 410 µs @8192. Causes the 256-tok transition bump; cutedsl still wins 1.56× at 8k despite it.
  • Exonerated: mma_fc1 (~60–70 µs, kernel_fc12.py:2324) is the fc1 weight-streaming bandwidth floor both backends share; sched_publish (~50–55%) and epi_fc1_wait (~40–48%) are pacing symptoms of it; Sched_PreInit_Wait ≈ 0 — dispatch arrival is not what compute waits on.
